10 Practical Ways to Save Money When Shopping Online

Originally published: 2024-10-24 · Updated: 10 November 2026 · By PriceEngine

Saving money online is less about finding a dramatic percentage-off banner and more about comparing the same item, checking the seller and understanding the total amount you will pay. These ten habits can help you make better decisions without relying on unverified discount claims.

1. Compare the exact product, not just the headline price

Search for the complete model name, storage capacity, colour and condition. A refurbished 128GB phone is not equivalent to a new 256GB model. Use PriceEngine to discover offers from different sellers, then confirm the listing details on the retailer's page before purchasing.

Example (illustrative, not live prices): Shop A lists a headset at £59 plus £6 delivery; Shop B lists the same model at £62 with free delivery. Shop B costs £3 less overall.

2. Compare the final checkout total

Add delivery, taxes where applicable, import charges and any compulsory fees. A lower item price may not be a cheaper purchase. For international orders, check whether VAT and duties are included and who pays return postage.

3. Check whether the seller is trustworthy

Read recent reviews, inspect return conditions and confirm the seller's identity. On marketplaces, a familiar platform name does not mean every individual seller offers the same service. Be cautious about unusually low prices, vague model numbers and payment requests outside the marketplace.

4. Check the product's condition

New, open-box, used and refurbished listings can look similar in search results. Look for battery-health information on phones, included accessories, cosmetic grading and warranty terms. A cheaper used item may still be the right choice, but compare like with like.

5. Plan purchases around your needs

Seasonal promotions can be useful, but a sale is not a guarantee of the lowest available price. Make a shortlist before major shopping events and compare offers on the day rather than relying on crossed-out prices.

6. Test valid voucher codes carefully

Look for codes on the retailer's own website or established voucher services. Check minimum spend, exclusions, expiry dates and whether the code removes another discount. A code that cannot be applied at checkout is not a saving.

7. Consider cashback, but read the terms

Cashback may be excluded for certain products, coupon combinations or payment methods, and it may take time to confirm. Treat cashback as a potential bonus, not money you are guaranteed to receive.

8. Compare warranties and return costs

Before buying expensive electronics, review the retailer's current warranty terms, statutory consumer rights and the cost of returning an unwanted item. Policies can change and can differ by seller, product and country.

9. Consider refurbished or older-generation products

For phones and laptops, a reputable refurbished offer or a previous-generation model may meet your needs for less. Check software support, battery condition, accessories and the seller's warranty. Avoid assuming a stated percentage saving applies to every product.

10. Set a budget and a decision rule

Decide what you need before searching. Compare at least two genuinely equivalent offers, calculate the delivered price and choose a seller whose terms you understand. If none fits your budget, waiting is a valid option.
