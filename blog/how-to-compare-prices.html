How to Compare Prices Online: A Practical Buyer’s Checklist

Originally published: 2024-10-20 · Updated: 10 November 2026 · By PriceEngine

A meaningful price comparison starts with the exact same product and ends with the full amount you will pay. Here is a repeatable method you can use for phones, shoes, home appliances and other online purchases.

Step 1: Identify the exact product

Write down the manufacturer, model number, size or storage capacity, colour and condition. A similar-looking item can have a different specification. For electronics, use the manufacturer's model identifier where available.

Step 2: Gather comparable offers

Search PriceEngine to discover listings from multiple stores. Check whether each result leads to the exact product or a broader search-results page; when it is a search page, choose the correct item before comparing. Remove accessories, parts, bundles and mismatched variants from your shortlist.

Step 3: Calculate the delivered cost

Use this simple formula:

Total purchase cost = item price + delivery + compulsory fees + applicable taxes or import charges − confirmed discounts.

Example using illustrative figures: Offer A costs £120 plus £8 delivery, while Offer B costs £125 with free delivery. If both are equivalent and no other fees apply, Offer B is £3 cheaper.

Step 4: Review the seller

Check the seller's name, recent ratings, location, dispatch estimate and contact information. For marketplace listings, seller terms may differ from the marketplace's general help pages. Never assume a low price means an authentic product.

Step 5: Compare returns and warranty coverage

Check the latest terms for the exact item and seller. Some products are excluded from standard change-of-mind returns, and return postage can affect the true cost. Legal consumer protections also vary by country and purchase circumstances.

Step 6: Evaluate condition and bundled items

A used phone with reduced battery health is not equivalent to a new phone. Likewise, a laptop bundle with a charger and manufacturer warranty may be better value than a bare device. Note what is included in each listing.

Step 7: Check whether a discount is genuine

A crossed-out price is not proof of a bargain. Compare the current delivered price with other sellers and, where available, independent price-history information. PriceEngine is a price-comparison discovery tool; this article does not claim that PriceEngine itself provides price-history charts.

Step 8: Make the decision

Choose the offer with the best combination of total cost, seller reliability, condition, delivery and after-sales support. The lowest advertised price is not always the lowest-risk purchase.

Quick checklist

  • Exact model and condition match
  • Current price verified on seller page
  • Delivery, taxes and fees considered
  • Seller reputation checked
  • Warranty and returns understood
  • Product is not an accessory or parts-only listing
